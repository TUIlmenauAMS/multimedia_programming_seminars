1. From Oscillator to Instrument: the ADSR Envelope

A raw oscillator produces a steady tone that starts and stops abruptly. Real instruments do not work that way — a piano note rises quickly, sustains while the key is held, and decays when it is released. This shape is modelled by an ADSR envelope, which controls the amplitude of the sound over four phases:

  • Attack — time for the sound to rise from silence to full volume after a key is pressed.
  • Decay — time to fall from full volume down to the sustain level.
  • Sustain — the level held while the key remains pressed (a level, not a duration).
  • Release — time to fade to silence after the key is released.

In the Web Audio API, the envelope is applied by scheduling AudioParam ramp methods on a GainNode. The signal chain for this synth is:

OscillatorNode → GainNode (ADSR) → AnalyserNode → AudioContext.destination

Each key press creates a fresh OscillatorNode and applies the envelope on the gain. On release, the release ramp is scheduled and the oscillator is stopped after it completes.

3. How It Works

Because an OscillatorNode can only be started once, a new one is created for every note. The gain envelope is applied using linearRampToValueAtTime for attack and decay, and setTargetAtTime for the release tail — this gives a smooth, natural-sounding shape without clicks.

Tip: Try a long attack and long release with the Sine waveform for a pad sound. Short attack and short decay with Square gives a plucky, percussive tone.
