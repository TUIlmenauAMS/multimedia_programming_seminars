Demo 1: Binaural Lab — Pure HRTF

An anechoic chamber rendered in Three.js. A single spatial audio source orbits the listener on a 3D path. Use OrbitControls to position your view. Toggle between HRTF Binaural (full spatial audio with ITD, ILD and pinna cues) and Stereo Pan (flat amplitude-only panning) — the perceptual difference, especially on the Elevation Sweep, is immediately audible with headphones.

Choose the audio source: a real evacuation alarm (broadband, strong transients — optimal for HRTF perception), pink noise (continuous broadband reference), or a harmonic stack (tonal, good for timbre changes with elevation).

CONCEPT: Head-Related Transfer Function (HRTF). The HRTF encodes how sound from every direction is filtered by the listener's head, pinnae, and torso. It is direction-dependent: the same source sounds different from above vs below, front vs behind. Resonance Audio applies a measured HRTF database via convolution. The key perceptual cues are ITD (interaural time difference — µs of delay between ears), ILD (interaural level difference — dB), and pinna spectral notches above 4 kHz that encode elevation.
CONCEPT: Why the alarm is the best demonstration source. HRTF perception requires energy across the full spectrum. The alarm has strong transients (optimal for ITD perception — the sub-ms onset makes interaural delay immediately perceptible), broadband energy (optimal for ILD), and energy above 4 kHz (required for pinna/elevation cues). Pink noise is a good continuous reference. A pure tone fails all three criteria.
CONCEPT: HRTF vs StereoPannerNode. StereoPannerNode is a pure amplitude pan law — no ITD, no elevation, no pinna spectral shaping. Switch to Elevation Sweep mode and toggle between HRTF and Stereo Pan: in Stereo Pan the source sounds identical at all elevations. In HRTF mode, elevation shifts are immediately perceptible. The 300 ms crossfade uses linearRampToValueAtTime to avoid click artifacts.
CONCEPT: OrbitControls for spatial audio exploration. With a free camera you can position yourself to observe the source from any angle — directly above, from the side, or at ear level. This makes the trajectory geometry and the HRTF sphere coloring much easier to interpret. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, right-click to pan.
CONCEPT: setFromMatrix() is the production pattern. resonanceSource.setFromMatrix(sourceMesh.matrixWorld) reads the full world-space transform from the Three.js scene graph — position AND orientation — in one call. It is correct even if the mesh is inside a transform hierarchy. setPosition() alone would miss orientation and desync from the visual.
